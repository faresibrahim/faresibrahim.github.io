// Overview

Safety, in every screen.

Aman (أمان — "Safety" in Arabic) is a thoughtfully designed mobile application for women who have experienced abuse, assault, or trauma. The design process was entirely research-driven — no screens were designed until user interviews were conducted, personas were built, and competitive products were analyzed.

The result is a calm, safe, and empowering digital space where users can process their experiences, find community, and access professional support — all through an interface that respects their privacy and emotional state.

// Design Process
01
Research & Discovery
User interviews with target demographic, desk research on trauma-informed design, and surveys to validate assumptions
02
Personas & Empathy Maps
3 detailed user personas capturing different user needs, emotional states, and usage contexts
03
Competitive Analysis
Evaluated 5+ existing mental health apps — identified gaps in safety features, Arabic support, and trauma-specific flows
04
Information Architecture
Card sorting exercises to define navigation, and user flows mapped for core journeys
05
Wireframes → High-Fidelity
Low-fidelity sketches iterated to polished screens with a calming rose & lavender visual language
06
Interactive Prototype
Fully clickable Figma prototype covering all core user flows — shared on Behance as a complete case study
// App Features
📔
Private Journaling
Secure, mood-tracked journal entries — a space that belongs only to the user
🫂
Support Circles
Anonymous community groups by shared experience — peer connection without identity exposure
👩‍⚕️
Expert Consultations
Book private sessions with licensed psychologists and trauma-specialized counselors
🌅
Daily Positive Notes
Affirmations and healing exercises delivered daily — building emotional resilience over time
🆘
Crisis Resources
One-tap access to emergency contacts, crisis hotlines, and safety planning tools
🔐
Privacy-First Design
Discreet app icon, quick-exit button, and biometric lock — safety at the OS level
